2 · The plan, written before the code

Full text in docs/WEEK-4-PACKET.md, committed five minutes before the first code commit.

Four brand decisions I made before the agent wrote anything

  • The headline test: my current brand headline, “Your stylist, in your pocket”, against “You already have something to wear”, which comes from what a real customer told me in Week 2 (both were in Spanish until October 26; see §18). I would rather learn which one works than keep the one I like.
  • The persona: a woman aged 24 to 30 in Mexico City — explicitly a hypothesis, because I have not interviewed anyone with that profile yet.
  • The channels: Instagram and TikTok.
  • The content: the agent drafts it using only verified data; I approve it before it is published, and the page says so.

The agent added one rule nobody asked for: do not use the Week 2 interview quote in social media. My mother's consent was for the research page and the document, not for advertising.

Problem

The old homepage said a nice phrase and little else: a visitor could not tell in ten seconds what problem it solves, what it costs, or why to believe it — and nothing built in three weeks, the sourced research, the pricing, the privacy work, appeared on it. ClosetAI also had nothing to go out and find anyone with: no written voice, no persona, not one post. And its headline had been chosen by taste, never measured.

5 · Architecture and stack

Browser (/ and /marketing — no session)
   │
   ├── /  (Server Component, dynamic)
   │      reads the cai_visitante cookie; if missing, creates a random id
   │      variant = hash(id + experiment) → top bit     ← pure function, tested
   │      └── <Experimento> (client): saves the cookie, records the view,
   │                                   records the click on “Start for free”
   │
   ├── /marketing (Server Component)
   │      brand, persona, content and A/B results from Postgres
   │      └── copy / export in the browser (no server needed for a CSV)
   │      └── generator → Server Action → Gemini (with fallback) → zod contract
   │                       + rejection of invented figures → saved as “not reviewed”
   ▼
Supabase Postgres
   ├── marketing_assets  (28 approved pieces + generated posts; public read)
   ├── ab_experiments    (the experiment and its variants; public read)
   └── ab_events         (one view and one click per visitor; server only — 401 to the public key)

What counts as a visitor: four filters, each from a real reason

FilterWhy it exists
The server recomputes the variant from the id and drops events that disagreeOtherwise anyone could inflate a variant by sending events by hand
One view and one click per visitor, enforced by a database constraintSomeone reloading ten times would otherwise move the result
Requests with the x-cai-prueba header are ignoredThe test suite runs against production; every run would add dozens of visits that are nobody's
Automated browsers, crawlers and link previews are ignoredVercel opens the homepage after every deployment to take a screenshot; each time it counted as two visitors (§13)

Why the variant is not assigned in proxy.ts

It is the natural place for the cookie, but it is also where the session is refreshed, and in Week 0 a small change there made sessions drop in production with every test green. The alternative costs less: the first visit picks the variant on the server and the browser stores the cookie.

PieceChoiceWhy
AssignmentFNV-1a + MurmurHash3 finalizerDeterministic, no dependency — and the finalizer is not decoration (§13)
SignificanceTwo-proportion z-testThe standard test for comparing two conversion rates; 30 lines, no library
GeneratorGemini 3.5 Flash, Flash-Lite fallbackAlready integrated, with the saturation handling from Week 2
GuardA closed list of verified facts + a number scannerThe prompt asks; the code enforces
ExportBlob in the browserA CSV needs no server; a BOM so Excel reads the accents

6 · The homepage, upgraded

Each visitor sees one of two headlines, chosen on the server, and keeps it on every return.

Below the headline the page reads as an argument: the problem, how it works in three steps, the data with its source (linked to /research), the price — free forever or 100 pesos once, linked to /pricing — and what happens to your photos.

Two sentences from the old homepage were removed because they could not be defended: “Two weeks in, it stops suggesting what you hate” was never measured, and “Ten garments are enough to start” contradicts the Week 2 interview, where the limit before getting tired was 40. A test checks they do not come back.

17 · Human decision note

This week I had to market a product, and the hardest part was not saying more than I can prove.

I chose to test my own brand headline, "Your stylist, in your pocket", against a phrase that comes from a real customer: "You already have something to wear." I would rather learn which one works than keep the one I like.

I rejected a post that said the virtual try-on works without your face. It had never been tested, and checking it meant uploading a photo of my body just to publish one sentence. I removed the post and corrected the product page, which had made the same promise since week three.

I also corrected three mistakes. My tests had not been running against the live site since September; now they are, and they pass. Vercel's screenshots after every deployment were counting as visitors in my experiment; I filtered them and deleted ten fake visits. And the closing line of the homepage repeated headline B, so people in group A were reading it too.

The tradeoff: my experiment will probably not have a winner by the deadline. It needs a hundred visitors per headline, so the page says "no winner yet" instead of showing a percentage that looks like a decision and is really noise.
